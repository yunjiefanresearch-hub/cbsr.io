why it is directional

One border. Two regulatory paths.

Destination requirements and origin-side issuance conditions jointly shape each corridor. Evaluating the two directions separately reveals their different authorization paths.

56 of the 66 jurisdiction pairs in this snapshot differ by direction. The directed-edge model preserves these distinctions across all 132 corridors.

The 20 August 2026 snapshot classifies US → EU as clearing and EU → US as in transition. The US commencement status accounts for that difference; each result remains tied to the snapshot’s date and research assumptions.

Six classes of corridor feasibility

Each class identifies the legal condition shaping a corridor: authorization, recognition, origin-side constraints, commencement, prohibition or the development of a regime.

Class What it means Legal condition
Category I Clears Each end is separately authorizable; this direction requires no additional equivalence determination in the model.
Category II Gated Access depends on a destination-side recognition, equivalence or comparability determination.
Category III Unresolved The origin lacks an exportable, authorizable token under the model. Partnership or coordination arrangements require separate analysis.
Category T In transition The destination has adopted a comprehensive regime awaiting commencement. The class is reassessed when it becomes operative.
Blocked Blocked at destination A destination-side issuance prohibition prevents an inbound corridor under the model’s assumptions.
Pre-regime Pre-regime A stablecoin regime is not yet in force at the destination. The model records this separately from prohibition and scheduled commencement.

why transition and pre-regime are separate states

Transition identifies an adopted regime with a commencement path. Pre-regime identifies a destination where the relevant framework is still being developed. The separate states support more precise planning and comparison.

Legal events over time

compose(as_of=…) evaluates corridors against the enactments and commencements recorded in the dataset. The scheduled events below show how legal changes can alter a corridor’s class.

Date Trigger Edges affected Change
2027-01-18 GENIUS Act §18 comparability gate commences. This is the outer date: it commences then, or 120 days after final rules issue, whichever comes first. 8 → US Category T → Category II
2027-10-25 The UK regime becomes operative (SI 2026/102). 8 → UK Category T → Category I

The evidence behind a corridor

Each result links the classification to its mechanism, primary provision, legal status, evaluation date and dataset version.

The class

One of six named states describing the corridor’s legal conditions.

The mechanism

The applicable mechanism: an equivalence determination, investor gate, prohibition or scheduled commencement.

The provision

The primary instrument and specific article supporting the analysis.

Legal status

The instrument’s commencement status is recorded as a separate field and checked by the build.

The date it was read

The as_of date identifies the legal snapshot used for evaluation.

The version

The version identifies the underlying dataset. Corresponding archive identifiers are listed in the repository.
