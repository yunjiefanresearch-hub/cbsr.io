KYA · Know Your Agent  ·  working framework v0.1, open for comment

Know the agent behind the action.

Financial agents bring a new set of operational questions to identity and due diligence. The KYA working framework organizes them into four areas: identity, authorization, capability and accountability.

4 questions  ·  4 assurance levels  ·  working framework v0.1  ·  open for comment

framework status

KYA is an independent research proposal, published as a working draft for discussion. It has not been adopted as law or by a standards body.

The dataset labels this material claim_class = proposed_framework and keeps it separate from the legal-evidence subset.

an operational view of the agent

KYC and KYB establish the customer or business behind a relationship. KYA adds an operational view of the software acting within it: the configuration, delegated permissions, tools and evidence used for a particular action.

Customer and business context
  • 01Identify the customer or legal entity responsible for the relationship.
  • 02Record the responsible party’s legal powers and delegated authority.
  • 03Understand the regulated activities and services within scope.
  • 04Retain records supporting subsequent review and accountability.
Agent operating context
  • 01Identify the model, process and configuration active for each action.
  • 02Record permissions and changes in a versioned authorization record.
  • 03Track the tools and interfaces available during execution.
  • 04Keep contemporaneous source and execution records for later review.

The four KYA questions connect this operating context to the person or institution responsible for the agent.

Four questions for financial agents

Each question addresses a distinct aspect of assurance. Keeping them separate lets a reviewer assess the supporting evidence for each one.

Q1

Identity — is this the same agent?

Bind a stable identifier to the agent’s model version, tool set and configuration. Record changes so transactions can be linked to the system that performed them.

The test: can someone point at a transaction afterwards and name the configuration that executed it, in a form a third party can verify.

Q2

Authorization — who permitted this, and how far does it go?

Trace delegated authority to a responsible person or legal entity. Record the scope and limits in a form that can be inspected before execution and reviewed afterwards.

The test: a structured authorization record specifies limits, counterparties, jurisdictions and the permitted time window.

Q3

Capability — what it can do, and what it cannot

Assess available tools and data coverage alongside the authority granted. A payment workflow may have access to broader interfaces, while its regulatory sources cover only selected jurisdictions.

The test: the agent declares its coverage and escalates requests beyond that boundary for further review.

Q4

Accountability — can what it read be reconstructed afterwards?

Retain the rules and sources used at the time of execution. Later review should be able to reconstruct the model, configuration and regulatory material relevant to the action.

The test: each execution records the source, version and as_of date, with an archive reference for the material used.

L1 – L4: the same questions, answered to what depth

The four levels describe progressively stronger evidence available to an independent reviewer. They assess verifiability across the four questions.

L1asserted

The agent states what it is

Identity, authority and capability are documented through self-declaration. External evidence has yet to be attached.

Answers: part of Q1. Does not answer: the extent of Q2, the boundary in Q3, any of Q4.

L2bound

Identity is bound to a responsible party

A stable agent identifier links to a named responsible person or legal entity. A structured record documents delegated authority.

Answers: Q1 and Q2. Does not answer: behaviour at the Q3 boundary, or the Q4 reconstruction.

L3bounded

Capability and coverage are explicitly bounded

Declared data coverage and tool limits determine how the agent handles requests. Requests beyond those limits are escalated for review.

Answers: Q1, Q2, Q3. Does not answer: Q4, months later.

L4reconstructible

The rules behind each execution can be fetched back unchanged

The execution record identifies the sources, version and as_of date used. Archived material supports reconstruction during a later review.

Covers Q1–Q4, including reconstruction of the evidence used for execution.

where this register sits in the framework

CBSR contributes to the data aspects of L3 and L4 through coverage fields, review status and versioned regulatory records. Agent identity, delegated authority and execution controls belong to the implementing institution.

scope and development

This draft sets out a research framework for discussion. Its scope and next steps are described below.

Working draft

Version 0.1 is published for independent review and discussion.

Research proposal

The L1–L4 model is proposed by this research. Applicable legal duties must be assessed in each relevant jurisdiction.

Conceptual design

The current draft defines four questions and four assurance levels. Implementation, protocol and schema design are future work.

Open development

Comments on the questions, levels and supporting evidence are welcome through the issue tracker.
