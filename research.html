Working papers

Six working papers develop the register’s analytical framework, spanning regulatory comparison, constraint interaction, corridor feasibility and evidence design. Five are published; the corridor atlas is in preparation.

The Multi-Jurisdiction Stablecoin Compliance Matrix
The cross-jurisdictional reference the register is built on: six regimes across ten regulatory dimensions, each entry sourced to primary law.
38 pp · SSRN ↗
Narrowing the Section 404 Prohibition
Behind one of the two doctrinal spines: five NAV-integrity conditions and four on-chain metrics that separate user-initiated routing from disguised yield under the US CLARITY Act's Section 404.
28 pp · SSRN ↗
Cross-Border Stablecoin Architecture
The constraint-interaction framework: eight constraints, six interaction sets, and a pairwise compatibility analysis that treats cross-border compliance as a composition problem.
85 pp · SSRN ↗
Cross-Border Digital-Finance Corridor Atlas
The directed-edge model and the six composite feasibility classes that turn the compatibility analysis into corridors.
in preparation
Cross-Border Stablecoin Feasibility Over Time
A trigger-conditioned forward map: how a corridor's verdict changes on the date a pending law commences, and a typology of triggers by the kind of certainty each carries.

Citable by Construction
The evidence model underneath all of it: three orthogonal axes, a build-enforced citable subset, and computed-versus-authored reconciliation.

Analysis

Dated analysis follows the legislative record, with a 2026 series examining Section 404 of the US CLARITY Act, related procedural questions and joint rulemaking.

  1. 15 Jun 2026

    Three Mechanisms, One Policy: Monetary Sovereignty in Cross-Border Stablecoin Regulation

    A transaction cap, a professional-investor gate and an outright prohibition, read as three instruments serving one policy.

    Duke FinReg Blog ↗
  2. 27 May 2026

    Reves' Fourth Factor and Stablecoin Routing Across Six Jurisdictions

    Whether the fourth factor directs Section 404 to step back when the routing destination is a fund registered under the Investment Company Act — and whether the parallel-track architecture that reading preserves is a US idiosyncrasy or a structural feature of Reves-style regimes.

    8 pp · PDF
  3. 23 May 2026

    A Stablecoin Yield Rule Goes to Three Agencies

    With the comparator phrase fixed at the 14 May markup, the substantive negotiation ends and a joint SEC / CFTC / Treasury rulemaking begins. What the two prongs actually hand the agencies is harder than the public debate suggests.

    3 pp · PDF
  4. 21 May 2026

    The Regulatory Discount on Token Launches Is Closing

    Of 118 tokens launched in 2025, 84.7 per cent trade below their listing valuation. Four frameworks now define a perimeter inside which one narrow class of launches can shed its implicit enforcement discount — and which side of it a token sits on decides what a high-FDV launch can defend.

    5 pp · PDF
  5. 19 May 2026

    Three Currency Areas Are Forcing Stablecoins to Split

    Why a single dollar-anchored rail cannot serve the European Union, Hong Kong and mainland China at scale. The yield prohibition leaks vertically; the architecture problem runs horizontally, and the horizontal version may matter more.

    7 pp · PDF
  6. 15 May 2026

    What the Reed–Smith Procedural Ruling Settled About Section 404

    The amendment that would have replaced the operative comparator phrase was ruled out of order. What survived is the word “solely” in the trigger clause — and the joint rulemaking now starts from a fixed comparator and a working “solely”.

    5 pp · PDF
  7. 11 May 2026

    When Wallets Route Into Registered Funds

    A user taps to route 500 USDC into a registered government money fund and signs. Is that signature a yield event the prohibition reaches, or a securities transaction it leaves alone? The question has a settled doctrinal home.

    5 pp · PDF
  8. 6 May 2026

    Narrowing the Section 404 Prohibition — written input to the SEC Crypto Task Force

    The author's proposed threshold NAV test, four on-chain metrics, and Committee Report language; listed on sec.gov on 7 May, not an SEC position or endorsement.

    28 pp · sec.gov ↗
