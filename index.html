the proof  ·  one border, two directions, two answers

EU→US

clears (Cat I) gated (Cat II) blocked regime in transition

Feasibility is directional. The binding rule sits at the destination's inbound gate, with a drag from the origin. Today US → EU clears and EU → US does not, though nothing bars it: the US regime is written and not yet in force. Move the date past 18 January 2027, the outer cap on the GENIUS Act §18 comparability gate (it commences on that date, or 120 days after final rules issue, whichever comes first), and that edge resolves to Category II along with seven others into the US. In this register 56 of the 66 jurisdiction pairs read differently depending on which way the money moves. A flat compliance table has one cell per pair. That is the whole problem.

the thesis

Software split by domain: one tool for HR, one for marketing, one for finance. AI is splitting the same way. Anywhere a wrong answer costs money, the model will need a record it can cite. Fluency is not evidence.

Regulation is where that gap bites hardest. Ask a general model whether a stablecoin flow is legal and it will answer smoothly. The text it is paraphrasing may have moved since it was trained, and it has no way to know. Here a confident wrong answer costs a licence. CBSR is the record underneath the answer: sourced, verified, versioned, and exposed to agents through typed tools, so a model reasons from a citation it can point at.

why it matters

Stablecoins now move real cross-border volume, and in 2026 the rules arrived at once: the US GENIUS Act, the EU's MiCA, Hong Kong's licensing regime. Whether a given flow is permitted depends on how two regimes compose at a border, and the answer moves on the day a pending law commences.

Today that answer lives in law-firm memos. A digital-assets partner in London or Hong Kong bills north of $1,000 an hour to write one. It arrives as prose, it is stale a quarter after you buy it, and no software can read it. Chain analytics goes the other way: it tells you where the money went, never whether it may go there. CBSR sits in that gap. The same judgment, written as data, with the citation still attached.

what it is

A dollar-referenced stablecoin at retail scale meets legally different instruments in different places: a transaction cap in one, a professional-investor gate in another, an outright prohibition in a third. Which one binds a given flow is decided at the destination's inbound gate, so cross-border feasibility is directional, and it moves as regimes take effect.

Twelve jurisdictions, read deeply. Each record is one verified (jurisdiction × instrument × dimension) fact, pinned to a primary instrument with a pinpoint, dated, version-stamped, and carrying its own evidence grade. Two hundred countries would mean a scrape, and a scrape cannot tell you which rule binds. The value here is the judgment in the data.

how it is built  ·  each layer derives from the one before it

01

Obligation records

The base facts. One sourced (jurisdiction × instrument × dimension) proposition of law per record, schema-validated.

02

Analysis layer

The pairwise compatibility matrix and the six constraint-interaction sets: the composition analysis, turned from prose into queryable data.

03

Corridor layer

Directed origin → destination edges, each read at the inbound gate, carrying its feasibility class and the mechanism that gates it.

04

Computed layer

compose() re-derives each corridor's class from a per-jurisdiction signal table and diffs it against the authored analysis. Disagreements are surfaced as findings, not silently fixed.

05

Time engine

compose(as_of=…) applies every scheduled change in law, so a corridor can read one class today and another once a regime is operative. US → UK flips to a clean Category I on 25 Oct 2027.

06

Constraint substrate

The deepest layer: a corridor's class composed from each jurisdiction's stance on eight constraints (C1-C8), through the interaction-set rules rather than a single inbound gate.

why you can trust it

Software reads a field and treats it as fact. It has no eyebrow to raise. So every judgment a careful human reader would keep to themselves has to be written down here, and enforced.

Every record carries three orthogonal axes: what kind of claim it is (a proposition of law versus a market fact), how well it is sourced, and whether the instrument is actually in force. Their intersection is the subset a lawyer or supervisor can cite as current binding law:

claim_class = tier1_legal ∧ status = in_force ∧ evidence_tier = resolution_text  →  46 citable records

The build enforces the discipline in CI: a record can't claim confirmation it has no source for, a market fact can't enter the instrument a lawyer is shown, and a statute that is written but not yet commenced can't be cited as current law. A rule engine re-derives the downstream conclusions and flags where it disagrees with the authored analysis. And the unverified backlog is published as queryable data, because the gates are defenses, not a cure. Only primary-source verification retires that liability, and it is never faked.

for the software that reads regulation

The register ships as a single dataset.json and an MCP server that exposes it to agents through roughly thirty typed tools: query a jurisdiction, compare a dimension, compose a corridor at a given date, or pull the lawyer-citable subset directly. Continuous integration runs the validation, the invariants, and the negative tests on every change, so an invalid record or a corridor that contradicts the matrix cannot be merged. A general-purpose model can call these tools and answer from the register, with a citation, instead of guessing.

status · what is not done yet

The register is at v0.10.x, depth-first over twelve jurisdictions rather than broad, and maintained by one person. A set of records is confirmed at regime-status level but not yet against the official text; that backlog is published as a per-cell worklist, not hidden. Regulation moves: Taiwan's regime passed at third reading on the afternoon this was last built, so the register is versioned and dated rather than evergreen. What it offers is not coverage of everything, but a verified, citable, machine-queryable account of the corridors it does cover.
